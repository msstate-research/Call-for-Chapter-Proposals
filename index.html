Guidelines for Chapters

1. Chapter Length

Your chapter should be up to 7,000 words, or about 28 double-spaced pages, including references, figures, and tables. References will appear after each chapter, as opposed to at the end of the book.

2. Chapter Format and Expectations

  1. Use double spacing, Times New Roman 12-point font, 1-inch margins on all sides, and left alignment.
  2. Keep a close eye on your allotted manuscript word length as you write.
  3. Limit the number of tables and figures to a maximum of 3 per chapter, if necessary. If you use pictures, save them as JPEG files at a minimum of 300 dpi.
  4. Use U.S. American punctuation, spelling, and grammar.
  5. Use APA style, 7th edition, for citations and references. For information about APA style, see Purdue OWL’s APA Formatting and Style Guide .
  6. Obtain permission for all materials borrowed from outside sources.
  7. DO NOT use screenshots or low-quality graphics.
  8. Limit the use of jargon and avoid a style that is overly academic.

Important: Chapters not following these expectations will be sent back to authors.

3. Audience

The intended audience includes teacher educators and professional learning facilitators, in-service teachers, including those in graduate programs, school and teacher leaders, graduate students, and researchers interested in place-based education and multilingual pedagogy.

This edited volume is intended for an international audience of educators and researchers.

4. Use of AI

Emerald Publishing’s stance on AI tools and authorship can be viewed through Emerald’s relevant guidance, and their Generative AI usage key principles should also be reviewed.

The use of AI is not permitted for copywriting, reporting, or the submission and publication of images created or edited by AI tools. Please ensure the manuscript follows these guidelines.

5. Chapter Structure

The structure for each chapter should include the following sections:

  • Introduction/context
  • Theoretical/conceptual framework
  • Research methods
  • Findings
  • Practice
  • Conclusion/implications

Chapters that are conceptual or practice-focused rather than empirical may combine or omit the methods and findings sections as appropriate.

Additional Author Guidelines

  • Number the pages of each chapter from 1 within each file.
  • Add a line between paragraphs and avoid indents at the start of a paragraph.
  • Do not use hyphenation to break words across a line, except in words where a hyphen is grammatically required.
  • Keep file names short and simple to avoid confusion and errors transferring and saving. File names should have a maximum character count of 30 characters, not including the file extension. Suggested format: AuthorLastName_Chapter
  • Please do not use special characters in any file names.
  • Please supply any diagrams or figures in black and white/monochrome.
  • Please provide high-resolution images suitable for use in the finished book, with a minimum resolution of 300 dpi.
  • Please supply images in separate picture files, preferably in .jpg format. Label images and figures clearly with a number and caption. For example: Figure 2: Multilingual Learner Enrollment Trends, 2023–2026

Submission Guidelines

Submit the following as separate files:

  • Chapter Draft: about 28 double-spaced pages total and no more than 7,000 words.
  • Figures: each figure should be submitted in a separate file in its original format. Add a callout in the chapter for each figure, such as INSERT FIGURE 1 HERE.

To streamline the process, please upload your file to the folder in the shared Google Drive labeled with your name. Do not email the chapter. Simply notify the editor once your chapter has been uploaded.

Note: Acceptance of a chapter proposal or positive review does not guarantee acceptance of the final chapter. Authors of accepted proposals must submit a draft manuscript by the deadline; each submission will be carefully reviewed. The editor and publisher, guided by peer-review recommendations, reserve the right to decline publication if the completed chapter does not meet the publisher’s academic standards.
